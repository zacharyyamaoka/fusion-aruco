Auto size on a clicked face: the largest clear rectangle

Click a face and the default size now comes from the largest rectangle that is clear of its outline, holes and notches, not from the face's bounding box. Ten faces, each sized offline and by the real add-in in Fusion, all checked against a brute force.

Code: CADExporter/bam_cad_core/aruco_autosize.py (fit_on_loops, largest_clear_rect, face_default). Faces: ArucoMarkers/aruco_autosize_gallery.py. Tests: tests/test_aruco_autosize.py.

In short

His Ø53 disc now defaults to a 28.0 mm marker, a 37.3 mm sticker (marker + a 1-module white border) in its 37.4 mm largest clear square. Before, the 70 % rule ran on the bounding box (37.0 mm), was shrunk to 27.5 mm by a coarse search, and said “shrunk for the face shape: 37.0 mm does not fit”.

One rule on every face: a single marker's sticker fills the largest clear square (never exactly flush); a grid or ChArUco board follows the doc's 70 % rule inside the largest clear rectangle. In Fusion, 30 of 30 tags built through the face-click path match the offline numbers exactly.

Why 28 and not 37: the square he sees is the sticker, and OpenCV needs the black square plus at least one module of white around it. 37.4 × 6/8 = 28.05, so 28.0 is the largest 0.5 mm step. A 37 mm black square does fit inside the disc (its diagonal is 52.3 mm), but its white border would fall off the rim at the corners. Size still means the black square; the dialog now shows both numbers.

How the default is found

  1. The face's loops, outline and holes, stroked the way Fusion strokes an edge (0.05 mm chords).
  2. The largest clear rectangle of a given aspect is found exactly, not sampled. A w × h box centred at p is clear iff no boundary segment comes nearer than h/2 in the norm max(|dx|/a, |dy|), so the best box is twice the deepest point in that norm. A branch and bound over square cells finds that point with a proven bound (0.02 mm): a cell can do no better than its centre's distance plus its half-size, so most cells are dropped unseen (Mapbox's polylabel method, in the box norm).
  3. The largest of all: 33 aspects from 1:16 to 16:1, the best refined by golden section, then each side pushed out by bisection to a maximal clear rectangle (an L's leg comes out exactly 20 × 60).
  4. Single: the largest 0.5 mm marker whose sticker fits the largest clear square, never exactly flush (5 µm). A flush white tile makes Fusion merge its walls with the body's; the live test caught this on a 40 mm plate. The step above is tried and refused exactly: a “does it fit?” search dives toward the best centres, so a 50 mm tile in a 50 mm gap is found (50 nm contact tolerance).
  5. Grid / ChArUco: the doc's size-first rule (default_layout, 70 % of the short side) on that rectangle, the board centred in it. If the largest rectangle holds no valid board, the next largest is used.
  6. A plain rectangle is its own largest clear rectangle: the same rules on the box (face_default), and the same for a rectangular sketch area.
  7. A typed size is kept when any placement holds it, with as many markers as fit (count first). If none fits, it is clamped to the largest step that does, with a note.

Every fit, auto or typed, takes 29 ms on average and 248 ms at worst (51 fits, pure Python, the same code Fusion runs). His disc: 24 ms single, 136 / 135 ms grid / ChArUco.

Checked against a brute force

The reference shares no code with the solver: each face is rasterised at 0.1 mm (pixel centres, even-odd over every loop), and every window of every size is tested with a summed-area table. It can be off by about a pixel, so it gives a band: “surely fits” (a window one pixel bigger all round is inside) to “may fit” (one pixel smaller). The test suite asserts that every auto and typed result lies in the band, which is within one 0.5 mm step. It also asserts that every tile is inside its face and clear of the holes (an independent dense-sample check), that the largest clear rectangle matches the raster's within 3 %, and that a face turned by 90°, 180° or 270° gets the same answer.

In Fusion

The bridge action autosize_gallery builds every face three times in a new document of its own, extrudes it 3 mm, makes an ArUco Extrude on the top face by the same path a click takes (aruco_feature.create with auto size, inside in_command), captures an orthographic top view with the joint, Joint Origin, sketch and origin folders hidden, then closes the document and puts the user's back.

  • 30 of 30 results equal the offline ones, counting the two faces where nothing printable fits a ChArUco board (the ring, the 14 mm strip): offline says ok = False, and Fusion refuses with the same note.
  • 25 are live ArUco Extrude features. The others are ChArUco boards as wide as the face (the L's leg, the plate beside its hole, the notched plate): their inlay splits the top face, so the live feature cannot hold on to it and the tag is made as a timeline group. This was already true on rectangles (a 6 × 2 board on a 20 mm strip), where it used to fail outright. create() now reads the face's token before the cut, so the tag is still made.
  • The end-to-end live_test (ten tags across faces, sketches, a child component, parameter, upstream and structural edits, colours, Joint Origins) passes after these changes: 39 of 39 steps, Joint Origin verdict PASS. Its colour checks are what caught the flush sticker.

In the dialog

  • Info line: “marker 28.0 mm · sticker 37.3 mm (white border 1 module = 4.67 mm)”. A board says “marker … · sticker W × H mm”; ChArUco says “marker … · checker … · board W × H mm (no outer border)”.
  • Size tooltip: Size is the black square (OpenCV's marker length); the white border, the quiet zone in Advanced, comes on top: the sticker is Size + 2 modules. Size keeps its name and its meaning.
  • Auto size tooltip: from the face's largest clear rectangle.
  • Reset in Advanced: a short label (“Defaults”) and the button's own text (“Reset”), not the same words twice. Not yet seen in his Fusion.
  • The Black height arrow limit is left to the height rework that follows this.

What changed

  • Non-rectangular faces: the default comes from the largest clear rectangle instead of the bounding box; no “shrunk …” note for an auto default.
  • Single on any face or sketch area, rectangles too: the sticker fills the clear square, so a 40 × 40 face gives 29.5 (sticker 39.3), not the doc's 28.0 (70 %). Boards keep the doc's rule; default_layout itself is unchanged, and so is its doc table.
  • Exact search: the cell-sampled largest_rect (≈ 0.44 mm cells) is replaced, inside the fitter, by the branch and bound. A typed 8 mm grid on the L now gets 6 along the 60 mm leg; the sampling found 5.
